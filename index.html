THE PLAYERS' GUIDE

Build a throw you can repeat.

A practical library for stance, grip, release, flight and finishing. Explore one topic at a time, then test it on the board.

There is no single perfect throwing style. Use these as experiments, not compulsory positions. The coach cannot see your mechanics.

Technique library

Tap a topic to see the diagram, details and a simple practice test.

◈Stance & balanceThree common stances, shown clearly

There are three recognisable ways to stand at the oche. None guarantees better accuracy. Choose a position you can repeat without wobbling, stretching or forcing your upper body.

Read the diagrams: Top-down view. The board is above the horizontal oche line. The rounded tip of each shoe marks the toes. The bright lead shoe stays behind the oche; its angle changes between the three stances. Right-handed setup shown; left-handers mirror the positions.

Side-on

Position: Lead foot nearly parallel with the oche; hips and torso more side-on.

Who it may suit: Suits players who feel stable with their throwing-side shoulder closer to the board and can turn comfortably without twisting or tension.

Watch for: Avoid forcing your torso to face the target if it strains your back, hip or neck.

Angled

Position: Lead foot turned partway across the oche, usually around 30–60°.

Who it may suit: A useful starting point for many players: combines a natural sightline with the support of the rear foot.

Watch for: Experiment with the angle; 45° is an example, not a required position.

Front-on

Position: Lead foot points more directly at the board, with the body facing forward.

Who it may suit: May suit players who prefer a more open chest position and find a sideways stance uncomfortable.

Watch for: Keep your weight settled and avoid rocking forwards during the throw.

Try it: Throw five three-dart visits at the same target from each comfortable stance. Compare grouping, natural shoulder alignment and balance. Choose one that feels repeatable and practise it consistently before changing again.

For all three: Keep the lead foot behind the oche, let the rear foot steady you and avoid losing balance as the dart leaves your hand. How much weight and forward lean feels comfortable varies between players.

✦Grip & barrel contactRepeatable pressure and finger positions

Grip tightly enough to control the dart but loosely enough to release without a deliberate flick of the fingers. Aim to contact the same parts of the barrel every time.

Finger placement depends on barrel shape and balance. A comfortable grip is more valuable than copying another player's fingers exactly.

Try it: Throw 15 darts with normal pressure, then 15 with slightly lighter pressure. Check whether your release and grouping stay consistent.
↗Throwing arm & releaseSmooth movement, repeatable finish

Use a controlled draw-back and a smooth forward action. Let the hand continue naturally towards the target after the dart leaves your fingers.

Keep your starting position and tempo reasonably repeatable. Arm paths and release points differ between players, and a forced straight-line movement can be counterproductive.

Try it: Throw three visits focusing only on the same pre-throw pause and follow-through. Then return your attention to the target.
◎Aiming & groupingControl your routine, not every dart

Choose a precise target and keep your eyes there as you throw. A clear aiming routine is more repeatable than changing technique after each miss.

Look at clusters over several visits: a consistent group away from the target is different from darts scattered unpredictably.

Try it: Record ten three-dart visits at 20. Note whether the misses tend to land in 1, 5 or elsewhere. Treat the pattern as information, not proof of a mechanical fault.
➤Dart flight & setupPoints, barrels, stems and flights

Flight shape, stem length, barrel balance and point length can change how a dart stabilises and enters the board. The same setup can behave differently for different throwing styles.

Change one component at a time, use enough throws to judge it and prioritise consistent grouping over a particular entry angle.

Try it: Compare two stem lengths over several identical three-dart visits. Record grouping and comfort before making a permanent change.
⑳Checkouts & board managementPlan your next dart before throwing
EXAMPLE · 61 LEFTT15 → D845 + 16 = 61
ALTERNATIVES25 → D1825 + 36 = 61

Consider the double you prefer and the score a miss might leave. A good checkout route isn't simply the shortest mathematical combination: it should be playable for you.

When a dart hits the wrong segment, recalculate from the actual remaining score. Avoid leaving one point or a finish that isn't available.

Try it: Play finishes 41–61. Before every visit, name your preferred route and one alternative if the opening dart misses.
◷Rhythm & match pressureMaintain the same routine under jeopardy
Settle›Focus›Throw›Reset

Pressure changes attention. Keep your routine short and repeatable: settle your feet, pick the target, throw at your normal pace, then reset for the next dart.

A failed visit doesn't mean the routine was wrong. Judge changes over many sessions, especially when pressure differs.

Try it: Give yourself three darts at a double for five rounds. Use the exact same pre-throw routine whether the previous round was a win or a loss.
